# Environment notes

Internal current-state notes for the Round 2 environment: dataset sources and their normalisation, the parts and the vault, prompts and scoring, the training stream, probe and evaluation sets, and the census. The design is `docs/design/round-2.md`, the contract between work packages `docs/dev/ROUND2_SPEC.md`; verifiers and sandbox are described in `docs/dev/VERIFY_NOTES.md`, the engine in `docs/dev/ENGINE_NOTES.md`.

## Code and commands

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `evotaste/sources.py` | one loader per source: rows of the cached dataset normalised to items |
| `evotaste/external.py` | parts, de-duplication, filters, and the guarded accessor `items(source, part, receipt, suites)` |
| `evotaste/tasks.py` | `Task`, `Score`, answer instructions, `prompt_ids`, `score_many`, procedural families, `Suites`, probe and evaluation sets, `Streams` |
| `scripts/make_suites_r2.py` | writes `configs/suites-r2.yaml`, the single source of the part assignment |
| `evotaste/census.py`, `configs/census.yaml` | the census of the base model |

All commands run from the repository root after `source scripts/env.sh`.

| Step | Command |
|---|---|
| Fill the dataset cache (once, through the mirror) | `python scripts/make_suites_r2.py --download` |
| Assign parts | `python scripts/make_suites_r2.py` (5 to 45 minutes depending on the machine's load, mostly the reference checks with 32 verifier processes) |
| Census, one part and shard per GPU | `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=<g> python -m evotaste.census run configs/census.yaml --part <part> --shard i/n` |
| Census summary and strata table | `python -m evotaste.census summarize configs/census.yaml` |
| Tests | `python -m pytest tests/test_tasks.py tests/test_external.py tests/test_census.py` (5 to 25 minutes; they build the parts) |

Everything after the download reads the dataset cache offline. Building the parts takes one to seven minutes depending on the machine's load (loading, copy detection, tokenisation). The first process to build them writes the train and selection parts to `results/cache/parts/<key>.json.gz` (outside git; safe to delete), where the key is the SHA-256 of the suites file and of the building code (`sources.py`, `external.py`, `tasks.py`, `verify/wrappers.py`); later processes with the same key read that file in seconds. Either way the parts are checked against the suites file's hashes. The cache never holds a vault item or id. Its key does not cover the dataset cache, which is fixed once downloaded.

## Parts

Every item belongs to exactly one part: `train`, `selection` or the vault `blind2` (Round 1's second vault, extended). The assignment, implemented in `external.py`, has three steps.

1. **Provisional part.** Round 1's own function, copied verbatim, decides GSM8K, MATH-500 and MMLU-Pro: foundation goes to train, blind1 (spent) to selection, blind2 stays in blind2. Other held-out items of training sources are halved between selection and blind2 by a seeded hash of their text (of the theory for ProofWriter). DeepScaleR and KodCode, which have no test split, give 5% each to selection and blind2, drawn only from items with no copy in another source, so that DeepScaleR's copies of MATH-train problems do not pull those problems out of training. An unseen unit goes whole to the part a seeded draw gave it: within each domain the units are shuffled and alternate between the parts, so the halves differ by at most one unit. No units were judged near-identical beyond the BBH tasks already merged into one unit each.
2. **De-duplication** over all sources at once, on the letters-and-digits string of a text (lower case, everything else removed, LaTeX sizing such as `\left` and `\dfrac` normalised), so that delimiters, spacing, punctuation and option formatting do not matter. Two items are copies when their problems are identical up to case and whitespace; or, between different sources or parts (`is_copy`), when their problems are identical in letters and digits, when their compared texts have the same sequence of numbers and either a Jaccard similarity of at least 0.5 between their character 8-grams or an edit similarity of at least 0.9 between their letters-and-digits strings, or when those strings are near-identical (edit similarity at least 0.97) whatever their numbers (an ARC question differing in one digit from a vault item with the same answer). The compared text is the stem (the problem without options or fixed wording), or for a choice item whose stem has fewer than 80 letters and digits ("Which of the following is true?") the whole problem with its options. Candidates are the pairs that MinHash with a well-mixing hash (splitmix64; 32 bands of 3 rows) finds at Jaccard 0.4 or more (measured recall 0.87 at 0.4, 0.99 at 0.5, 0.998 at 0.6); the exact measures then decide. The number condition exists because similarity alone does not separate copies from template siblings: open pairs at or above 0.5 with the same numbers are mostly the same problem reformatted or reworded, while pairs with other numbers stay siblings even above 0.8 (`What is the largest prime factor of 78?` against `of 999?`, J = 0.81; `x^2+12x=73` against `x^2+14x=33`), and a sibling with other numbers is kept. Below 0.5 with the same numbers, reworded copies (`Suppose that the angles of triangle ABC satisfy \cos 3A + \cos 3B + \cos 3C = 1 ...` against a rewording, J = 0.43) sit next to siblings sharing numbers (`a, b, c` against `a, b, c, d` nonnegative with sum 1, J = 0.43); the edit test removes the closer of them (the ordered triples with `a^2 b + b^2 c + c^2 a`, J = 0.41, edit 0.95), and also siblings as close as `the sum of the possible values` against `the product` (edit 0.90). Within one source and part only the first kind counts, because there items sharing a context (questions on one theory) or differing only in symbols (BBH bracket sequences, Boolean expressions, which are all alike in letters and digits) are distinct items. Of two copies the one in the higher part survives (blind2 over selection over train); within a part the copy of the smaller source survives, which keeps the roughly 600 TheoremQA problems that recur in MMLU-Pro in TheoremQA. Omni-MATH items with a copy in DeepScaleR are removed whatever their parts.
3. **Filters**: no gold; free-text gold (an open reference with `\text` or `\mbox`, longer than 40 characters, or with a bare word of three or more letters such as `9 and -7` or `Saturday`, which the verifier would read as a product of symbols matching any anagram); a prompt over 3,500 tokens (none at present); a reference that does not verify against itself in every wrapper of its kind (`gold_check`; for code, the reference solution failing its own tests). A reference check that times out or loses its verifier process is repeated up to three times; one that always times out counts as failed, and a verifier process that keeps dying stops the build (an infrastructure failure is never a verdict). Every rejection is confirmed by a second check, because under heavy load a parse can fail without reporting a timeout (8 TheoremQA references were rejected once at a load average of 70 and passed on recheck).

An open reference shaped like a list is typed for the verifier, which refuses untyped ones: half-open brackets, `\infty` or `\cup`, or the word "interval" in the problem make it an `interval`; parentheses otherwise a `tuple`; a bare list a `tuple` when the problem asks for an order, an ordered pair or coordinates, a `list` when it asks for all solutions or values separated by commas; TheoremQA's lists are tuples. A list-shaped reference that none of these decides (a closed `[a, b]` without the word interval, a bare list without a cue) stays untyped and is dropped by the reference check.

Removal never depends on a verdict: de-duplication runs before the filters, on every candidate. `configs/suites-r2.yaml` records the verifier's `RULES_VERSION` (loading refuses a file generated under other rules), the unit draw, which sources have items in which part, the procedural families, per source and part the item count and the SHA-256 of the sorted uid list, the counts of every drop reason, and the uids of train and selection items whose reference failed. Loading recomputes the parts and refuses any mismatch with the recorded hashes. Vault items are listed nowhere: their reference checks are rerun when the vault is opened, and the result must reproduce the recorded hash. The generator and the loaders read vault texts only to hash and de-duplicate them; after loading, a process keeps only train and selection items.

`external.items(source, "blind2", receipt)` and `tasks.blind_suite` require the receipt that only `evaluate.blind_guard` issues, as in Round 1; `fixed_tasks(..., "blind2", receipt=...)` passes it on. `Suites` never reads the vault's units. Error messages that could name vault items give counts only.

`configs/vault-exposure.yaml` (from `scripts/vault_exposure.py`) lists, as SHA-256 hashes of uids only, the vault items on which, or on a copy of which (`copies`, one step), a stored model output exists, or once existed, anywhere under `runs/` and `results/` (a rerun keeps the previous record's hashes, so deleting old outputs hides nothing); `external.exposed(uids)` tells the blind evaluation which of its items these are, so that the opening reports every result with and without them. Since vault membership can be recomputed from the code and the data, the hashes hide nothing from a person; they keep the ids out of the file. Rerun the script whenever outputs on new items are stored outside an opening, and before the opening.

## Prompts and scoring

The system prompt is the single sentence `tasks.SYSTEM_PROMPT`; a seeding context is appended to it after a blank line (`prompt_ids(..., context=text)`). The question is the problem, a blank line, and the answer instruction of its kind and wrapper, built from the wrapper's instruction sentence (`evotaste.verify.WRAPPERS`):

| Kind | Default wrapper and instruction |
|---|---|
| open | box ("Put your final answer within \boxed{}."); the verifier's program route is not offered (direct channel only) |
| choice | "Answer with the letter of the correct option." and box |
| verdict | "Answer with exactly one of: …" (the item's labels) and box |
| code | code block, "It must define …" (the declaration, the function name, or the starter code's class `Solution`) |
| procedural | box |

`rewrap(task, wrapper)` puts the same problem under another wrapper (a guardrail variant or a held-out wrapper: that wrapper's instruction). `score_many` maps a task to the verifier's job (kind, text, gold, wrapper, the direct channel) and sets the reward to 0 unless the completion stopped; a program in a response is never an answer, so `channel` is always "direct" for an answered open task. Procedural tasks are scored here: reasoning-gym families by their own scorer in eight persistent scorer processes (Round 1's machinery, with its time bounds), exact-match tasks inline; the reward needs the wrapper asked for, `correct_any` accepts the content of any answer wrapper, which gives the format probe its strict and lenient scores.

The verifier can also read an open answer from a printed Python program, but the instruction does not offer that route. Offered it in an earlier smoke, the base model answered by program in 0.2% of open answers and appended an ignored program after its box in 60%; forced to answer by program, 23% of its programs printed nothing, and accuracy on sweet-spot items was 0.23 against 0.40 for forced direct answers. Under the box-only instruction no open answer contained a program, and on the same 30 items per source the pass rate rose from 0.86 to 0.88 on GSM8K and from 0.68 to 0.72 on MATH, with open answers 12% longer.

## Training stream and seeding prompts

`Streams(suites, run_seed, strata=load_strata(path, sha256), version=v)`. The pools are the train-part items that the census stratified, one per source and stratum; a mastered or frontier pool with fewer than 50 items gives its share to the source's sweet pool, and a source with fewer than 50 sweet items is an error. A batch of P prompts is split first among the domains (math 2/5, code 1/4, logic 7/20), then within each domain among its source-stratum cells (equal shares per source; sweet 7/10, mastered 3/20, frontier 3/20), each split by largest remainder with the remainders carried from batch to batch and ties broken in a seeded order: every batch follows the domain shares to within one prompt, and over a run every cell gets its share to within two. Each pool is walked in a seeded permutation, without replacement until it is exhausted, then in a new one. Of the non-code prompts, 3/20 (cumulatively, to within one) carry a training-variant wrapper chosen uniformly; which prompts is seeded per step. Batch t is a pure function of (run seed, t, P, the suites file, the strata table, the version), whatever was computed before, so every member and every resumed worker gets the same prompts. The strata table must not name items outside the train part.

The version is the run's, recorded when it was prepared. In version 1 a cell that was rounded up carries a negative remainder, and when its next quota is smaller than that, it receives a count of −1: the batch then holds one prompt more than asked and the pool's next draw repeats an item (on pilot C's pools at 48 prompts, steps 13 and 32). Version 2 gives such a cell 0, carries its negative quota on, and balances the batch on the other cells; with the same tie-breakers it agrees with version 1 wherever version 1 has no negative count, and on pilot C's pools no cell's cumulative count strays by more than 0.92 from its share over 300 steps. Runs prepared before the fix (pilots B, C, D and the clones added to them) keep version 1.

`seed_batch(i, n, domains)` draws from the whole train part with the same domain and source shares (restricted to `domains`), default wrappers, its own seeded walk per domain set.

## Probe and evaluation sets

Both are independent of the run seed. The probe set takes the first `round(300 / (sources + 1))` items (13 at present) of the seeded order of every selection source, one instance of each procedural selection family (stream `probe`), and then 60 further items of the non-code sources, round robin, under the held-out wrappers in turn. `fixed_tasks(suites, part, per_source, per_family)` takes, per source, the first `per_source` items of the part's order after the probe items, and the first `per_family` instances of each procedural family in stream `eval`: the items the selection census covers, so an evaluation of up to about 230 items per source uses censused items only; a procedural draw whose generator raises or whose gold its scorer rejects is replaced by the position's next draw, as in Round 1.

## Census

`configs/census.yaml` fixes the model, the suites and contexts files, 4,096 tokens, chunks of 256 jobs, the vLLM settings (KV 12G, `gpu_memory_utilization` 0.42) and the three parts: train (8 samples on a seeded draw, the first items of the train part's order: 3,000 per math source, 2,500 per logic source, so all of ARC, and 7,000 of KodCode), selection (4 samples on the first 250 items of every selection unit, its probe and then its evaluation items, and the first 25 evaluation instances of each procedural family), and contexts (2 samples under each of the twelve contexts on the probe set's plain items). Requests are seeded by (part, uid, variant).

A chunk is written atomically to `results/census/<name>/<part>/chunk-NNNNN.jsonl.gz` once all its samples are scored; a rerun skips finished chunks, and shard i of n takes chunks whose index is i mod n, so shards can run on several GPUs and be re-sharded. Two chunks are in flight at a time so that the GPU stays busy while one is scored. A process refuses to start on a card with less than `need_free_mib` free, and refuses to write a chunk in which more than five samples (or 1%) lost their verifier or scorer process: such a chunk measures the infrastructure, not the model. The strata table refuses train records that are not exactly those of the current train part. Do not run the census, or the generator, while the verifier code in the working tree is being edited: verifier processes are fresh interpreters and pick up whatever code is on disk. `--limit N` (at most N items per source and family, output under `<name>-limitN`) is for smoke runs. Every record has the uid, source, kind, wrapper, context, the text, the completion length, the finish reason and every `Score` field. `summarize` writes `strata.tsv` (header `uid passes samples`, sorted by uid; frontier 0, sweet 1–6, mastered 7–8 of 8) and `summary.json`: per source and part the pass-rate histogram and strata sizes (train), accuracy, tolerant accuracy, compliance, truncation, errors and response-length percentiles, per context the same by domain, and the throughput per process. A run config pins the table's SHA-256.

The census reads items only through `Suites.items` for train and selection and the probe set, never holds a receipt, and checks every job's uid against the open parts; a test asserts all three.

Launch the full census as one process per GPU: `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=<g> python -m evotaste.census run configs/census.yaml --part train --shard <i>/4` for i = 0..3, `--part selection --shard <i>/2` and `--part contexts --shard <i>/2` for i = 0, 1, then `summarize`. The parts are independent and their shards can run side by side; a card with 30 GB or more free can take `--kv-cache 20G --gpu-memory-utilization 0.62`, which only changes speed.

**Measured on smoke runs** (2026-11-25, Qwen3-1.7B on one L20). With a 20G cache on a lightly shared card: 5,580 completion tokens/s on the train part and 5,250 on selection (`--limit 100`), 3,600 and 4,950 on a smaller run (`--limit 30`, whose train part is one full chunk and its long tail); with the default 12G cache on cards busy with other jobs: 2,000 to 3,000 tokens/s. A 12G cache holds only 100 to 250 sequences at a time (usage 99–100%), so the rate is set by the cache and by co-tenants (on a CPU-saturated machine vLLM's scheduler also starves), not by the model. Under the final instruction, mean completion lengths run from 116 (KodCode) to 1,640 (DeepScaleR) tokens per sample, p50 / p90 / p99 = 392 / 1,260 / 3,411 on the train part; truncation 0.3%. Scoring a chunk took 0–55 s, hidden behind the next chunk's generation; with the parts cache a process starts generating about a minute after launch, plus vLLM's 1–4 minutes. The full census with the caps above (253,432 samples, about 138 M completion tokens) projects to 7 GPU-hours at the 20G rates, 10 at the smaller run's, and 15 at the 12G rates.

## Known limitations

- **LeetCode's train split is old.** Its 2,600 problems date from 2015 on and are likely in the base model's pretraining data, unlike the test split's 228 from 2024-08 to 2025-03; the unit's selection score mixes the two (the split is visible in no field of the task, only in the dataset).
- **Copy detection is lexical.** Paraphrases, translations and rewordings that change more than half of the character 8-grams are not found, nor copies whose numbers differ in writing (`twelve` against `12`), and a rewrite with other numbers counts as a different problem. A copy kept by de-duplication can afterwards fail a filter while its removed twin would have passed.
- Omni-MATH loses 664 items to free-text references (equation lists and chains included) and keeps 189. The reasoning-gym format-probe families score near 0 on some families (integration, prime factorisation in LaTeX), since their own scorers are strict about notation; that is what the probe measures.
- MMLU-Pro counts as logic although it holds math and science questions; TheoremQA's `option` items are dropped rather than parsed.
- A process that builds the parts (no cache for its suites file and code) holds every source in memory while doing so, vault texts included, as Round 1 did; protection of the vault is the accessor's refusal, not memory isolation.
- Opening the vault reruns its reference checks; a verifier that changed since generation makes the hash check fail, and the suites file must then be regenerated before the vault can be read. The verifier now limits CPU time rather than wall time and asks code references to pass within half their limit, so verdicts depend far less on machine load than before (when KodCode moved by 3 items between two generations); every rejection is confirmed by a second check, the stored verdicts keep train and selection fixed, and a vault verdict that still differs at the opening fails loudly rather than changing silently.
